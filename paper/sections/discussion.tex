\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three principles transfer. Split the accounting state by the question it answers:
exact where the quantity is money and must be settled, one-sided where it has time
structure and must be cheap. Key every charge on the party that bears the loss, not
the one who pays. Treat the charge as a transaction with a commit point, or it will
eventually be incremented twice or not at all.

A deployment would replicate the coordinator over a few consensus nodes, own each
subject on one node so the critical section stays local, and shard the ledger by
subject range. That removes the single-process bottleneck but leaves a trust question:
a subject's budget lives on one node, which could under-report a charge. Signed
receipts make that detectable afterwards, which is weaker than the guarantee inside one
process; closing it needs Byzantine agreement or a proof that the charge matches the
mechanism executed.

The accounting layer is itself sensitive: the ledger records which subjects appear in
which queries, and the hot table is close to a list of the most-queried subjects, so a
reader learns participation patterns without learning any answer. Accumulators,
oblivious access or an enclave for the hot table each cost something the design does
not budget for; this is the most important open problem here.

Three further limitations bound the claims. The SLO monitor samples the hot table,
so its coverage depends on the hot capacity, and sampling the epoch ring directly is
a fix we have not evaluated. An adversary that observes throttling and
re-times its fragments is measured in E18: probing lifts its loss from 0.80 to 0.85 of
the cap and stops there, because the key still binds it. An adversary that cycles subjects through the
hot table is measured in E14: it costs accuracy and availability, never soundness.

Two conditions would falsify the approach. If a population is small and its policy
cumulative, exact counting is cheaper, as our ablation shows at $1{,}500$ subjects;
with one platform, a per-platform quota is already a per-subject quota and is sound. And if a query's cost cannot
be predicted before execution, admission must price conservatively or fall back to
post-hoc charging, weakening the guarantee to the one auditing provides. The design is
for what remains: many subjects, several platforms, many tenants, and a promise that
holds before the answer leaves the building.
