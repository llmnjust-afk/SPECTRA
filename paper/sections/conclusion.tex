\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Privacy budgets in data markets are enforced after the fact, keyed on the party that
pays, and reconciled outside the transaction that spends them. Each choice is
individually reasonable and together they fail: the wrong key lets a tenant split its
traffic, the wrong timing releases the answer before the overspend is known, and the
wrong data structure loses the charge on a crash. \sys\ moves the decision to
admission around three commitments---an exact ledger where settlement requires
exactness, a one-sided sketch where time makes exactness unaffordable, and a
two-phase reservation where atomicity separates a budget from a guess. On a million
subjects the estimator holds 4.7\,MB where exact accounting needs 265\,MB;
enforcement admits 0.93 of requests at $1.0\eps$ with no violations where per-tenant
blocking admits 0.98 and breaches the cap a dozen times; the escape ratio under a
splitting tenant stays at 0.82 where policies without a subject key reach 2.43. The
cost is measurable---4.4 points of availability at $1.0\eps$, 4.2 without
degradation, a striping benefit only for disjoint subjects---and we report it with
the gains.
The next step is to make the accounting state itself private, and to replace the
trust we place in one coordinator with a proof that the charge matches the mechanism
that ran.
