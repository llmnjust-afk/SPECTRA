\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Privacy budgets in data markets are enforced after the fact, keyed on the party that
pays, and reconciled outside the transaction that spends them, which lets a tenant split
its traffic, release the answer before the overspend is known, and lose the charge on a
crash. \sys\ moves the decision to
admission around three commitments---an exact ledger where settlement requires
exactness, a one-sided sketch where time makes exactness unaffordable, and a
two-phase reservation where atomicity separates a budget from a guess. On a million
subjects the estimator holds 4.7\,MB where exact accounting needs 265\,MB;
enforcement admits 0.93 of requests at $1.0\eps$ with no violations where per-platform
blocking admits 0.98 and breaches the cap a dozen times; the escape ratio under a
splitting tenant stays at 0.82 where policies without a subject key reach 2.43. The cost is measurable---4.4 points of availability at $1.0\eps$, 4.2 without
degradation, striping that pays only for disjoint subjects---and we report it with the
gains.
The next step is to make the accounting state private and to replace the trust in one
coordinator with a proof that the charge matches the mechanism that ran.
