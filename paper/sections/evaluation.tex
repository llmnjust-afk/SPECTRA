\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Methodology}
\label{sec:eval-method}

We run on a two-vCPU container with 4\,GiB of RAM and CPython 3.11; the host is
deliberately small, so absolute latencies are a conservative upper bound. Experiments
use three seeds and report the mean, except the cap sweep, which is rerun on twelve;
per-seed rows ship with the code.

The generator produces $12{,}000$ requests from $40$ tenants over $1{,}500$
subjects and three platforms, with popularity from a truncated Zipf law of exponent
$1.15$ and $15\%$ of requests touching several subjects; every subject carries a cap
of $\eps{=}1.0$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$. Identities are drawn by inverse transform, so
large ranks fold back onto small ones and the heavy hitters that make accounting hard
survive.
The splitting attack gives an adversary $12$ target subjects, a budget of
$0.30\eps$ per target and $k$ fragments from $12$ to $60$, issued under distinct
identities across platforms.

We compare against five policies: \emph{static quota}, a fixed per-platform
allowance that meters spend but can neither throttle nor window it; \emph{token
bucket}, a per-subject allowance that refills at $0.05\eps$ per second; \emph{audit only}, which admits everything and
flags subjects afterwards and is the deployed status quo; \emph{exact blocking},
per-platform counters checked against composed spend; and \emph{exact global}, exact
per-subject counters shared across tenants and platforms, with no window, breaker or
degradation. \sys\ is the only
policy that is subject-keyed across platforms, time-structured and memory-bounded in
its estimator.

\subsection{Is the Estimator Sound and Accurate?}
\label{sec:eval-accuracy}

Approximation must never produce a false negative: one at admission is a cap
violation. Across 24 configurations---four widths, with and without the hot table,
three seeds---we observe \emph{zero} false negatives. The property is not
statistical: it follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:onesided}, and the experiment
confirms the implementation respects it across eviction and re-admission.

Accuracy separates along the population. For the top percentile of subjects by
spend---the ones whose budgets bind---relative error is 4.3\% without a hot table
at width 512, 0.10\% with one, and 0.02\% at width 2048 and above, where the hot
table rather than the width does the work. For the tail, mean absolute error is
0.071$\eps$ at width 512 and 0.013 at width 2048, and p99 absolute error falls from
0.37 to 0.10. Width 32{,}768 is wider than the number of distinct keys here and measures zero
error at 2.1\,MB, which is why it is not the configuration we deploy.

\subsection{What Does It Cost?}
\label{sec:eval-cost}

The estimator costs 27--32\,$\mu$s per request across widths from $2^{10}$ to
$2^{16}$, against 4.6\,$\mu$s for an exact table on the same trace: an 18\% rise
for a sixteen-fold increase in width, as expected from eight memory accesses per
update plus the hot-table lookup. The full admission path is slower and far more variable
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}a): median 74\,$\mu$s, mean 223\,$\mu$s, p99 5.6\,ms.
Profiling attributes 46\% to the sketch and cost algebra, 14\% to the ledger, 12\%
to the controller and 7\% to the accountant, and the tail is a single function, a
tenant-pressure sweep that scans the hot table every 64 admissions. Disabling it
buys back half the mean and almost all of the tail.

Queueing decides the deployment (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}b): at 50\% utilisation the
median is 0.17\,ms but the p99 is 107\,ms, because one 5.6\,ms event stalls
everything behind it; a load that averages 50\% while peaking at five times capacity
makes the median 0.93\,s. The path must be provisioned for its peak.

Memory tells the opposite story at small scale: below roughly $10^{4}$ subjects a
dense table is no larger than the sketch, because the sketch's width is paid once
and the table's cost has not caught up.
Figure~\ref{fig:scale} measures the regime that matters, with the default ring
(four live tables) and resident memory: from $10^{3}$ to $10^{6}$ subjects the dense
table grows from 1.2\,MB to 1.2\,GB while the sketch stays at 10.6\,MB (E23).
Per-request cost rises more gently, from 25 to 45\,$\mu$s for the sketch and 9 to
13\,$\mu$s for the table; the estimate stays one-sided, with maximum absolute error
0.057$\eps$ (E8).

The structure answering windowed queries is therefore $O(1)$ in $N$, whereas a
naive windowed accountant---one cumulative structure per epoch---multiplies the
measured 1.2\,GB by the ring depth. The whole system is not: the ledger keeps one
scalar balance per subject that has ever transacted, far smaller than a cost vector,
and a deployment would shard it.
