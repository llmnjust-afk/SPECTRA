\subsection{Where Is the SLO Frontier?}
\label{sec:eval-slo}

Sweeping the soft threshold $\alpha$ from 0.55 to 0.95 at four values of $p$ moves
availability from 0.749 to 0.794 and raises the fraction of $(\alpha,p)$ settings
at which the SLO is satisfied from 0.25 to 1.0. The hard cap never moves: there are
zero violations at every setting, so tightening the soft target costs throughput
and never soundness. The knobs are not independent: at $\alpha{=}0.55$ the monitored violation rate is
2.2\%, so the SLO holds only for $p\ge5\%$, while at $\alpha\ge0.85$ it is at most
0.22\% and the default $p{=}1\%$ holds. Operators choose a tight target with a
loose rate or the reverse, 4.5 points of availability apart, not both: a small set
of subjects crosses any threshold near zero whatever the policy.

The sensitivity sweep varies one factor at a time around the default and uses a
single seed per point, so it locates trends rather than estimating their
variability. Two points bracket the aggregation effect. With one platform, static
quota becomes sound---zero violations, worst spend $0.969\eps$---because a
per-tenant quota on the only platform is also a per-subject quota; with six
platforms its worst spend rises to $1.859\eps$ while \sys\ stays at $0.825\eps$.
A heavier tail ($\alpha_{\mathrm{zipf}}{=}1.5$) costs 15 points of availability,
from 0.936 to 0.783; 200 tenants cost 4, as more of them touch each subject between
refreshes; a 64-entry hot table costs 2; and moving $\delta$ from $10^{-6}$ to
$10^{-4}$ raises availability from 0.920 to 0.941. Crashes at probability 0.05 cost
one point and produce no violations. Across all 19 points the violation count is
zero and the estimator never under-estimates.
