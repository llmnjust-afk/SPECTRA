\subsection{Where Is the SLO Frontier?}
\label{sec:eval-slo}

An SLO has two knobs, the soft threshold $\alpha E$ and the tolerated violation
rate $p$, and the interesting question is which pairs are achievable. Sweeping
$\alpha$ from 0.55 to 0.95 at four values of $p$, holding everything else at the
default, moves availability from 0.749 to 0.794 and raises the fraction of
$(\alpha,p)$ settings at which the SLO is satisfied from 0.25 to 1.0. The
hard-cap violation count is zero at every setting, including the tightest:
tightening the soft target degrades throughput and never compromises the cap.

The two knobs are not independent. At $\alpha{=}0.55$ the monitored violation
rate is 2.2\%, so the SLO is met only if the operator accepts $p\ge5\%$; at
$\alpha{=}0.85$ and above the monitored rate is at most 0.22\% and the default
$p{=}1\%$ is met. This gives operators a menu---a tight soft target with a loose
violation rate, or a looser target with a strict rate, at a cost of about 4.5 points
of availability---but not a tight target with a strict rate, which no policy can
deliver on a heavy-tailed workload: a small set of subjects crosses any threshold
near zero regardless of admission policy.

The sensitivity sweep varies one factor at a time around the default, with a
single seed per point. Two points are controls. With a single platform, static quota becomes sound
(zero violations, worst spend $0.969\eps$), because a per-tenant quota on the only
platform that exists is also a per-subject quota; with six platforms its worst spend
rises to $1.859\eps$ while \sys\ stays at $0.825\eps$. These two points confirm
that the baselines fail through aggregation rather than through an implementation
defect. A heavier tail ($\alpha_{\mathrm{zipf}}{=}1.5$) costs 15 points of
availability, from 0.936 to 0.783, because more traffic concentrates on the subjects
whose budgets bind; 200 tenants cost 4 points, as more of them touch each subject
between refreshes; shrinking the hot table to 64 entries costs 2 points; and moving
$\delta$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ raises availability from 0.920 to 0.941, since
a larger $\delta$ converts the same $\rho$ to a smaller $\eps$. Crashes at
probability 0.05 cost 1 point and produce no violations.
Across all 19 points the violation count is zero
and the estimator never under-estimates.
