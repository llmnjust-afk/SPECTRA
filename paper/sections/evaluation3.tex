\subsection{Where Is the SLO Frontier?}
\label{sec:eval-slo}

Sweeping the soft threshold $\alpha$ from 0.55 to 0.95 at four values of $p$ moves
availability from 0.749 to 0.794 and raises the fraction of settings in which the
\emph{monitored} violation criterion is met from 0.25 to 1.0, while the hard cap never
moves. The knobs are not independent: at $\alpha{=}0.55$ the monitored rate is 2.2\%,
so the criterion holds only for $p\ge5\%$, while at $\alpha\ge0.85$ it is at most
0.22\% and the default $p{=}1\%$ holds---a tight target and a loose rate are 4.5
points apart, and E25 shows where the population rate departs from the monitored one.


The sensitivity sweep varies one factor at a time with one seed per point, so it
locates trends, not variability. Two points bracket aggregation: with one platform, static quota becomes
sound---worst spend $0.969\eps$---because a per-platform quota on the only platform is
also a per-subject quota; with six, its worst spend rises to $1.859\eps$ while \sys\
stays at $0.825\eps$.
A heavier tail ($\alpha_{\mathrm{zipf}}{=}1.5$) costs 15 points of availability,
from 0.936 to 0.783; 200 tenants cost 4; a 64-entry hot table costs 2; moving
$\delta$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ raises availability from 0.920 to 0.941; and
crashes at 0.05 cost one point. Across all 19 points violations are zero and the
estimator never under-estimates.
