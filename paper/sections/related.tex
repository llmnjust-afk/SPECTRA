\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Privacy accounting.} Composition has spent two decades getting tighter, and
the trajectory is visible in recent work on continual observation, which separates
oblivious from adaptive adversaries~\cite{bun2026separating} and revisits the cost
of answering online queries~\cite{bun2016make}. Tight auditing has become its own
line, from reconstruction attacks on production query systems~\cite{cohen2018linear}
to Gaussian-DP audits of synthetic data generators~\cite{ganev2026tight} and
analyses of the gap between provable and auditable privacy in
DP-SGD~\cite{modi2026revisiting}. All of it asks how large the loss was; \sys\ asks
whether the next release may happen, which has a different error budget. We do not
improve a composition bound. We take a standard one, zCDP~\cite{bun2016concentrated},
and make it cheap and monotone enough to sit on an admission path, alongside
alternatives such as R\'enyi~\cite{mironov2017renyi} and Gaussian
DP~\cite{dong2021rejoinder} whose relative merits we measure for that
purpose~(Section~\ref{sec:eval-backend}).

\emph{Systems that enforce privacy.} PINQ established the pattern of a query
interface that charges a budget per analyst~\cite{ebadi2015featherweight}, and
production libraries apply it to training~\cite{yousefpour2021opacus}. These
systems enforce a budget per \emph{analyst} and assume the analyst is the unit of
privacy. In a market the unit is the subject and the analyst is an adversary who
can be several analysts, which is why the key and the cross-tenant aggregation
matter more than the query language. Our settlement protocol has no counterpart in
this line; the closest analogue is accounting in a database transaction.

\emph{Auditing and governance.} A parallel literature builds the machinery for
post-market monitoring~\cite{mokander2021conformity} and ethics-based
audit~\cite{mokander2021ethicsbased}, and empirical studies of organisational
practice~\cite{kapp2024collection} show how far declared policy is from measured
behaviour. Privacy-policy formalisms give declarations a
semantics~\cite{majedi2024privacy}, and recent work extends governance to
AI-mediated platforms~\cite{shouli2025ethical}. \sys\ is complementary: it assumes
the declaration exists and makes it enforceable at the point of sale, and an
auditor who can read a ledger of admissions need not infer what happened from the
released artifacts.

\emph{Data markets.} Trustless trading has occupied the blockchain systems
community, with recent designs scaling delivery and
settlement~\cite{liu2025yotta} and provenance mechanisms tracking derived
artifacts~\cite{zhu2025auditing}. These answer ``did I receive what I paid for?''.
The privacy question---``may this answer be sold at all?''---is orthogonal and, in
our reading of deployments, unaddressed: the ledger records payments, not budgets.
We keep the ledger as the settlement substrate and add a budget root that every
platform consults, the smallest change that lets the same infrastructure answer
both questions.

\emph{Approximate counting.} Count-Min sketches and their conservative variants
are well understood~\cite{fusy2023countmin,fusy2022phase}, including on power-law
streams and for external storage~\cite{goswami2018buffered}, and learned variants
reduce error at the same width~\cite{nishishita2025optimized}. Our use is unfashionable: we want not the smallest error but an error
with a known sign, which rules out unbiased estimators and makes the conservative
update the right primitive rather than an optimisation.

\emph{Heterogeneous budgets and mechanisms.} Charging subjects different budgets is
a recognised generalisation~\cite{alaggan2015heterogeneous}, and per-subject
constraints appear in genomic query answering~\cite{jiang2022answering} and
distance-preserving transformations~\cite{kenthapadi2012privacy}; these establish
that per-subject accounting is meaningful, not how to maintain it across tenants at
market scale. The answers \sys\ prices come from standard mechanisms, from DP
synthesis~\cite{saha2024dpcda} to spectral perturbation~\cite{feng2023spectraldp},
geometric preconditioning~\cite{bosch2026dpkfc,duan2025technical}, federated
training~\cite{han2022momentum} and private sensing~\cite{beugin2022building}.
We assume only that each query exposes a cost vector, as all of these do in some
form; calibrating to data~\cite{proserpio2012calibrating}, releasing under a
sensitivity budget~\cite{hardt2010simple} or handling high-sensitivity queries
through concentration~\cite{nissim2017concentration} changes the vector, not the
enforcement layer.
