\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

We state the properties the design guarantees and sketch why they hold. The proofs
are short because the mechanisms are chosen to make them short: one-sidedness is an
invariant of an update rule, safety of a check-and-record, atomicity of an
identifier.

\begin{proposition}[One-sidedness]
\label{prop:onesided}
For every subject $s$ and time $t$, the cumulative estimate satisfies
$\hat{\spend}_s(t)\succeq\spend_s(t)$, and the windowed estimate
$\hat{\spend}_s(t,W)$ satisfies $\hat{\spend}_s(t,W)\succeq\spend_s(t,W)$, where
the comparison is componentwise on $(\eps_{\mathrm{pure}},\rho)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
We maintain, for every layer $j$ and key $x$, the invariant
$\min_r A_j[r,h_{j,r}(x)]\ge T_j(x)$ where $T_j$ is the true charge. It holds with
all cells zero. On an update of $x$ by $c$, the conservative rule sets each cell on
$x$'s row to $\max(a,\,m+c)$ with $m\ge T_j(x)$ the current row minimum, so every
cell on that row becomes at least $T_j(x)+c$; cells of other keys only increase. The invariant is preserved. A subject held in
the hot table is seeded with the sketch's bound at re-admission, hence at least
$T_j(x)$ at that instant, and is then incremented exactly, so the invariant holds
there too. The windowed estimate sums whole epochs that cover the window, each
bounded below by its true charge, and charges are non-negative; hence the sum
bounds the true windowed charge, provided the ring holds at least
$\lceil W/\ell\rceil+1$ epochs, which Section~\ref{sec:design-accountant} enforces.
\end{IEEEproof}

Over-estimation costs availability; Section~\ref{sec:eval-accuracy} measures 0.02\%
relative error for the top percentile and no false negatives in 24 configurations.

\begin{proposition}[Cap safety]
\label{prop:safety}
Assume that every platform reserves through the shared coordinator before a query
executes, that the coordinator is the only writer of a subject's budget, and that
the cost it charges is the cost of the mechanism the platform executes. Then, for
every subject $s$ and time $t$, $C_s(t)+F_s(t)\preceq E_s$, the committed charge
$C_s(t)$ is non-decreasing, $F_s(t)\succeq0$, and no executed query leaves
$C_s(t)\succ E_s$---even when tenants split their traffic across accounts and
platforms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Let $C_s(t)$ be the committed charge and $F_s(t)$ the sum of live reservations.
Admission of $q$ at time $t$ requires
$C_s(t^-)+F_s(t^-)+\cost_{\mathrm{exec}}(q)\preceq E_s$ for every subject in the
support, and the test and the insertion of the reservation happen under one stripe
guard per subject, so no other transaction observes $C_s+F$ between them. A live
reservation of $q$ then undergoes exactly one of three transitions. Commit moves
$\cost_{\mathrm{exec}}(q)$ from $F_s$ to $C_s$, which leaves $C_s+F_s$ unchanged;
release removes the same vector from $F_s$; expiry removes it as well. Hence
$C_s(t)+F_s(t)\preceq E_s$ holds in every reachable state, $C_s$ never decreases
and $F_s\succeq0$, because a reservation leaves $F_s$ the moment it is settled or
expires. A query that executes carries a committed charge by
Proposition~\ref{prop:atomic}, and is charged once, so the loss it incurs is
counted in $C_s$ exactly once and cannot exceed $E_s$. Splitting does not enter
the argument because the key of every reservation is the subject, not the account
or the platform.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[Exactly-once settlement]
\label{prop:atomic}
Under crashes and retries, each admitted query is charged at most once, and no
answer is delivered without a committed charge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Commit is keyed by the transaction identifier and returns the existing record if
the transaction is already committed, so a retry cannot charge twice. The
platform calls commit before returning the answer to the tenant, so an answer
that leaves the platform has a committed charge; a crash before commit means the
answer is never delivered. A reservation that is neither committed nor released
is expired after its TTL, which bounds the budget held by a dead client. The
protocol can therefore over-charge a subject whose answer was computed but lost
to a crash, which is the safe direction, but cannot release data without
charging for it.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded instability]
\label{prop:stability}
For a single breaker key, the number of de-escalations in $T$ evaluations is at
most $\lfloor T/d\rfloor$, where $d$ is the dwell parameter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
A de-escalation requires $d$ consecutive evaluations below the current
threshold minus the hysteresis margin, and the counter resets on any evaluation
that is not below it. Each de-escalation therefore consumes at least $d$ distinct
evaluations.
\end{IEEEproof}

The bound is loose---E7 observes 12 de-escalations over roughly 5{,}000 evaluations
with $d=3$---but it has the right shape: transitions are controlled by a parameter
rather than by the load, and removing hysteresis raises the count to 26.

Two properties are deliberately absent. We do not prove that estimator error stays
below a constant as the population grows, because the Count-Min guarantee is
relative to total mass; we measure it instead. Nor do we prove that the SLO is met under
every arrival process: the breaker is a feedback controller, and an abrupt load
increase can drive pressure past the reject threshold before it reacts. The hard cap
survives such a transient because an exact structure enforces it.
