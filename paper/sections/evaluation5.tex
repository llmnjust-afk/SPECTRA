\subsection{Settlement and Concurrency}
\label{sec:eval-concurrency}

Six settlement invariants pass, among them the refusal to re-bind a reserve: the
identifier is bound to a fingerprint of tenant, platform, query, support, charge,
mechanism and policy version, and a per-identifier lock serialises its owners before the
stripes are taken, so two requests with disjoint supports cannot both insert a
reservation. A committed transaction replays its stored receipt, and a second commit
carrying a different receipt is refused. In the atomicity test, 16 threads issue 3{,}200
attempts against a subject whose cap funds 100 charges; every thread count admits exactly
100, $1.0\eps$ and zero violations.

Injecting a crash at each step of the transaction tests Proposition~\ref{prop:atomic}
directly (E19), with the invariants read off the ledger. Over every crash point, one and
four threads and a crash rate up to 5\%, no answer left without a committed charge, no
transaction was charged twice, and in-flight spend never crossed the cap: a crash after
reserve with a same-id retry delivers once and charges once, a retry after the TTL finds
the hold returned, and a crash after commit withholds the answer but keeps the charge.
The price is the crash rate, 188 of 200 delivered at 5\%, plus one request in 800 blocked
by a suspended hold; three unshared per-platform ledgers let a subject reach
$3.0\times$ its cap.

E19 keeps the ledger alive; E21 kills the process. Six SIGKILLs at the four settlement
steps deliver 389 answers, none uncharged, at 0.191$\eps$ against a $0.2\eps$ cap; the
same schedule on an in-memory ledger delivers 571 and pushes five of ten subjects past
the cap---a restart that forgets is a budget reset. Durability costs 76\,$\mu$s per
transition against 7.5\,$\mu$s. Striping pays only for disjoint subjects (E13, medians of
three): four threads reach 21.4k operations per second on one subject against 26.5k for a
global lock, and 91.5k on disjoint subjects---it removes a serialisation point, not
contention.

Three platform processes against a coordinator of their own reach about 230 answers per
second with each step fsynced (E22); killing that coordinator mid-trace and restarting it
from the log costs $\approx$1.1\,s and a few percent of throughput, and still delivers
760 answers, none uncharged, no duplicate charge, worst client spend $0.38\eps$ against
$0.5\eps$. One client over loopback sustains 1.9k pairs per second at a 201\,$\mu$s
median round trip. The caveats are one host, a loopback trip and a stub adapter that hides
the real commit trip.
