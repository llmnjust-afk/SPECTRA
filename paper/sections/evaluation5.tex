\subsection{Settlement and Concurrency}
\label{sec:eval-concurrency}

Five settlement invariants are checked directly and all pass: a repeated commit
charges once, a timeout releases, a repeated reserve returns the same reservation and
cannot be re-bound, a crash between reserve and commit leaves no double spend, and an
expired reservation is recovered. In the atomicity test, 16 threads issue 3{,}200
attempts against a subject whose cap funds exactly 100 charges; every thread count from
2 to 16 admits exactly 100, spends exactly $1.0\eps$, and records zero violations.

Injecting a crash at each step of the transaction tests
Proposition~\ref{prop:atomic} directly (E19). Over every crash point, one and four
threads, and a crash rate up to 5\%, no answer left without a committed charge, no
transaction was charged twice, and in-flight spend never crossed the cap. A retry
before the TTL reuses the reservation and charges once; a retry after it is refused and
the hold returns. The cost is the crash rate itself---188 of 200 requests at
5\%---plus one request in 800 blocked by a suspended hold. Three platforms with one
ledger each spend 3.0 caps.

Throughput depends on the workload, and our earlier reading was too pessimistic. With
every thread contending for one subject, striping buys nothing: four threads reach
21.4k reserve-and-commit operations per second against 26.5k for a single global lock,
and even one thread is 28\% slower. With disjoint subjects the same code scales: four
threads reach 91.5k against 25.2k, a factor of 3.6, because a transaction waits only
for the stripes it touches (E13, medians of three). Striping removes a serialisation
point, not contention: concentrated traffic will not benefit.

Served over loopback TCP, one client sustains 1.9k reserve-and-commit pairs per second
at a median round trip of 201\,$\mu$s; four sustain 2.4k at 283\,$\mu$s with a p99 of
16\,ms. The server saturates, so the limit is the interpreter, not the transport, and
more needs subjects sharded across coordinators, which we have not built. The caveats
are that the coordinator is one process, that the round trip is a loopback one, and
that the Fabric adapter is a stub: a real ledger adds a commit round trip we did not
measure.
