\subsection{Settlement and Concurrency}
\label{sec:eval-concurrency}

Five settlement invariants pass, among them the refusal to re-bind a reserve: the
identifier is bound to a fingerprint of tenant, platform, support and charge, so a
retry that changes any of them is refused rather than silently reused (tests, E22). In the
atomicity test, 16 threads issue 3{,}200 attempts against a subject whose cap funds 100
charges; every thread count admits exactly 100, $1.0\eps$ and zero violations.

Injecting a crash at each step of the transaction tests
Proposition~\ref{prop:atomic} directly (Table~\ref{tab:faults}, E19), with the
invariants read off the ledger rather than off the caller's bookkeeping. Over every
crash point, one and four threads, and a crash rate up to 5\%, no answer left without
a committed charge, no transaction was charged twice, and in-flight spend never
crossed the cap; the price is the crash rate, 188 of 200 delivered at 5\%, plus one request in 800
blocked by a suspended hold. The last row, three unshared ledgers, motivates the
shared coordinator.

E19 keeps the ledger alive; E21 kills the process. Six SIGKILLs at the four
settlement steps deliver 389 answers, none uncharged, at 0.191$\eps$ against a
$0.2\eps$ cap; the same schedule on an in-memory ledger delivers 571 and pushes five
of ten subjects past the cap, the worst to $2.1\times$---a restart that forgets is a
budget reset. Durability costs 76\,$\mu$s per transition against 7.5\,$\mu$s.

Striping pays only for disjoint subjects (E13, medians of three): four threads reach
21.4k operations per second on one subject against 26.5k for a global lock, and 91.5k
on disjoint subjects against 25.2k---it removes a serialisation point, not contention.

Three platform processes against a coordinator process of their own reach about 230
answers per second with each step fsynced (E22); killing that coordinator mid-trace
and restarting it from the log costs $\approx$1.1\,s and a few percent of throughput,
and still delivers 760 answers, none uncharged, no duplicate charge, and a worst
client spend of $0.38\eps$ against $0.5\eps$. One client over loopback sustains 1.9k
pairs per second at a 201\,$\mu$s median round trip. The caveats are one host, a
loopback trip, and a stub adapter that hides the real commit trip.
