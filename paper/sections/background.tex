\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Differential Privacy and Its Accounting}
\label{sec:bg-dp}

A mechanism $M$ satisfies $(\eps,\delta)$-differential
privacy~\cite{dwork2006differential} if for every pair of neighbouring datasets $D,D'$
and every event $S$, $\Pr[M(D)\in S]\le e^{\eps}\Pr[M(D')\in S]+\delta$. The
definition is per-release, but composition carries it to a transcript, which is what
lets a platform promise a bound on \emph{total} leakage rather than on each answer,
and why that promise needs bookkeeping. R\'enyi DP~\cite{mironov2017renyi} gives sharp
constants for adaptively chosen mechanisms, at the cost of a discrete order grid.
Zero-concentrated DP~\cite{bun2016concentrated} bounds the R\'enyi divergence of
order $1+\alpha$ by $\rho\alpha$ for all $\alpha>1$, composes by adding $\rho$,
and converts in closed form,
\begin{equation}
\eps = \rho + 2\sqrt{\rho\ln(1/\delta)} ,
\label{eq:zcdp}
\end{equation}
with inverse $\rho = \bigl(\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)+\eps}-\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)}\bigr)^{2}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:zcdp} is not the tightest known conversion---$f$-DP and
Gaussian DP sharpen composition for Gaussian mechanisms~\cite{dong2021rejoinder}---but
it is monotone, invertible and cheap, which is what an admission decision needs.

\subsection{The Market Setting}
\label{sec:bg-market}

We consider a market in which subjects contribute records to one or more
platforms and tenants buy answers over those records. A permissioned ledger records
contributions, charges and settlements, so a tenant can verify that an answer
corresponds to a committed query. Trustless
trading designs provide this for delivery~\cite{liu2025yotta}, and provenance
mechanisms extend it to derived artifacts~\cite{zhu2025auditing}. What the ledger does not provide is a statement
about a subject's remaining budget, because that depends on queries answered
elsewhere and on charges still in flight.

Three properties shape the design. The population is large and heavy-tailed: most
subjects appear in a handful of queries, a small minority in thousands, and it is
that minority whose budgets bind. Tenants are numerous and mutually distrustful,
with an incentive to maximise information per unit of budget, including under
several identities. Queries arrive continuously, so enforcement sits on the
critical path of every answer and has a latency budget of tens of microseconds.

\subsection{Approximate Counting for Thresholds}
\label{sec:bg-sketch}

A Count-Min sketch stores a $d\times w$ matrix of counters and updates, for key
$x$ and weight $v$, one cell per row at the positions given by $d$ independent
hash functions~\cite{fusy2023countmin}. The estimate is the minimum of the $d$
cells, so collisions can only inflate it. Conservative update strengthens the
guarantee: instead of adding $v$ to every cell it computes the current minimum
$m$ and raises each cell to at most $m+v$, which never lowers an estimate and
reduces overestimation in
practice~\cite{fusy2022phase,fusy2023countmin}. A space-saving summary over the
same stream retains the $k$ largest keys exactly and evicts the smallest. Recent work replaces the hash
functions with a learned model~\cite{nishishita2025optimized} and revisits the
row-count trade-off~\cite{fusy2023countmin}; both optimise estimation error,
whereas our requirement is weaker and, in one respect, stricter: the estimate
must never fall below the truth, because it gates a safety property rather than a
reported statistic.

\subsection{Service-Level Objectives}
\label{sec:bg-slo}

An SLO is a predicate over a measurement window, not over a lifetime total, and a
subject's tolerance is typically the sliding version: enforcing only the total
exhausts the budget in the first hour, enforcing only the window lets the total drift. \sys\ maintains both: a cumulative sketch that never forgets, and a ring of epoch
sketches for windowed queries, with the SLO on the window and a separate hard cap on
the total.
