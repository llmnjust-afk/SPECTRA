\subsection{Ablation and Stability}
\label{sec:eval-ablation}

Removing one component at a time (three seeds, E7): the full system admits 0.923 of
requests at 4.8\,MB and 100.9\,$\mu$s, exact global 0.929 at 0.24\,MB and
10.8\,$\mu$s. Removing degradation, so the breaker can only admit or refuse, costs 4.2
points of availability: degradation converts would-be refusals into answers. Shrinking
the sketch from $2^{14}$ to $2^{9}$ columns cuts memory to 0.71\,MB without moving
availability, and widening it only costs memory---on 1{,}500 subjects the hot table
holds everyone who matters.

Churn is the cost the capacity sweep hides (E14): rotating 400 heavy subjects per epoch
raises the mean over-estimate from 0.16 to 0.28 at $0.15\eps$ and from 0.25 to 0.65 at
$1.0\eps$, and the p99 from 1.7 to 5.1$\times$. Safety is untouched, since an evicted
subject is re-seeded from the sketch, but the table stops paying for itself.

Disabling the table raises availability to 0.930 but widens every windowed estimate.
That baseline is less comfortable than it suggests: at 1{,}500 subjects exact cumulative
accounting matches \sys's availability within a point (0.898 against 0.906 at
$1.0\eps$) with 0.38\,MB against 5.05\,MB in the same 600\,s configuration, and its
19\,$\mu$s runs no breaker, coordinator or window. A windowed exact accountant (E16)
admits every request at $0.5\eps$ and still lets six of 1{,}500 subjects exceed their
\emph{lifetime} cap, spending 1.08\,MB; at $1.0\eps$ it reaches 1.000 against 0.906 with
one lifetime violation. Putting the exact table behind the same ring, breaker and
coordinator isolates approximation from functionality (E20): availability and windowed
peak are equal at both caps, and the exact version is faster, 156 against
233\,$\mu$s, at 0.37 against 5.01\,MB. At $10^{3}$ subjects the sketch buys nothing but the slope; measured from $10^{3}$ to
$10^{6}$ with four live tables, it ends at 1.2\,GB resident against 10.6\,MB (E23).

E26 adds a sparse exact map---one ring per active
subject---and the settlement state behind all three, at $10^{6}$ subjects with four
epochs: 30.5\,MB dense, 1.6, 17.5, 83.8 and 168\,MB sparse at 1, 10, 50 and 100\%
activity, and 2.7\,MB as a sketch. Below about 2\% activity the sparse map is both
smaller and exact, and we do not claim the sketch there; by 10\% the sketch is six times
smaller. In the full system the exact ledger costs about 1.1\,KB per committed subject,
so at full activity the settlement state alone is 1.1\,GB and the estimator choice moves
the total by a fraction of a percent: the sketch earns its place in the window state,
not the settlement state.

On a load oscillating around the threshold, hysteresis and dwell give the breaker
zero transitions; without them, 26, of which 13 are reversals.

\subsection{Why zCDP and Not R\'enyi DP}
\label{sec:eval-backend}

The choice of algebra is about solvability, not tightness. A R\'enyi accountant
evaluates a discrete grid of orders and its conversion to $(\eps,\delta)$ floors at
$\log(1/\delta)/(\alpha_{\max}-1)$, which is $0.0225$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$ and
$\alpha_{\max}{=}512$: a tenant asking for $\eps_q{=}0.02$ sits below that floor, and
our implementation saturates at $\sigma{=}1000$, 4.17 times the zCDP scale, and refuses
the query rather than charge $0.023$. Basic composition reaches $0.2008$ after 100
charges at that target, ten times zCDP's budget; at $\eps_q{=}0.1$ and $0.5$ the three
backends agree to 0.004\% and 0.006\%. A R\'enyi grid extended to
$\alpha_{\max}\ge1{,}200$ would also calibrate $0.02$, at the price of a conversion
admission must search rather than invert.

\subsection{What Soundness Costs in Utility}
\label{sec:eval-utility}

The utility experiment puts a real dataset behind the cap: the UCI breast-cancer set
(569 records, 30 features), split 70/30 stratified into 398 training and 171 test
records. Features are clipped to the public unit interval, so the L2 sensitivity of a
mean vector is $\sqrt{30}/398=3.4\times10^{-3}$; each release is four Gaussian
mechanisms at a quarter of the per-release zCDP cost, the variance is debiased by the
released noise variance, and five seeds give the spread. At $\eps_q{=}1.0$ \sys\ admits one query at $0.87\pm0.06$; at
$0.5\eps$ three at $0.84\pm0.06$; at $0.25\eps$ fifteen at $0.60\pm0.22$. Cheaper releases do not buy accuracy: the admissible count grows as $1/\eps_q^2$, so
the $\sqrt{n}$ averaging gain cancels the $1/\eps_q$ noise. The baselines answer 60 or
100 queries at 0.92--0.93 while spending 2.0--12$\eps$, buying accuracy with a violation
in every seed; 13.7\% of admitted queries are throttled rather than refused.
